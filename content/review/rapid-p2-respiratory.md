Deck: `content/rapid/p2-respiratory.json` (307 cards, 17 class cards).
Sources: KD Tripathi 8e ch16 (chapter PDF, read in full via `pdftotext`). Extra cards from Katzung 16e ch20 (asthma and COPD chapter, owner's book) where KD 8e is silent or older, marked in `refs`.

## Corrected cases

None. Every KD ch16 statement checked was consistent with the owner's other books or with itself.

Checked and kept as printed:
- KD ch16 says montelukast is metabolised by CYP2C9 with CYP3A4 as well. GG14 (table of PK footnotes) says "metabolized by CYP3A4 and CYP2C9". Consistent, so no card was changed. No CYP card was made because it is not an exam-level fact.

## Omitted

- KD ch16 Table 16.2 (inhaled corticosteroid daily dose bands), fluticasone row: the extracted columns do not align cleanly to MDI and DPI, so no fluticasone dose card was made. Beclomethasone/budesonide and ciclesonide bands were carded because their column values read clearly.
- Theophylline dose-reduction factors ("age > 60 (0.6), CHF (0.6), pneumonia (0.4), liver failure (0.2-0.4)"): the garbled symbol before each number was read as a dose multiplier. Card wording states them as "dose factors" for that reason.
- Adrenaline receptor subtypes: the text uses garbled Greek symbols. Cards say "mixed alpha and beta agonist" and "beta1 and beta2 agonist" rather than reproducing the symbols.
- Brand names and product strengths (for example Asthalin, Duolin, Cadistin combinations) were not carded. Only the Duolin salbutamol-plus-ipratropium strength was kept because it is a clear dose fact.
- Caffeine pharmacokinetics and uses are in KD ch35 and were not carded here (the chapter 16 text cross-refers to ch35).

## Notes on book statements (kept as printed)

- KD ch16 says expectorants are no longer marketed by US-FDA except guaiphenesin. Carded as printed; this is a regulatory statement from KD only.
- KD ch16 states that montelukast/zafirlukast are not for terminating attacks, and Katzung agrees. No conflict.
- KD ch16 says ICS have been advocated as "step one" for all asthma patients, but are currently not considered necessary for mild episodic asthma. The ICS indication card follows the current indication (persistent asthma); the step-one advocacy was not carded.
