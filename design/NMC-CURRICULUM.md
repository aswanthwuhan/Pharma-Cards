## 3. Practical / clinical examination

**Source: nmch.ac.in copy (2020), section "PRACTICAL / CLINICAL AND ORAL / VIVA VOCE EXAMINATION". Total marks: 400.**

| # | Exercise (as listed) | Marks |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Long Case | 150 (inferred from column order; sums to 400) |
| 2 | Short Case | 100 (inferred) |
| 3 | Spotting (25 x 2) | 50 (inferred) |
| 4 | Viva voce | 100 (inferred) |

The marks column is not clearly aligned in the extracted text. The four values are 150, 100, 50 and 100, and they sum to 400. Confirm the allocation against the NMC PDF.

**Long experiment (as listed):** demonstrating drug effects or interpreting results in an anaesthetised animal, plus a table exercise. Table-exercise examples:
- calculating pharmacokinetic parameters
- statistical exercise
- critical appraisal of a published paper (abstract writing)
- evaluation of drug literature
- protocol design
- ADR reporting and causality assessment
- assessment of preclinical toxicity data
- analysis of rational and irrational formulations

**Short experiment (as listed):**
- (a) isolated tissue experiment (bioassay, per Government regulations), or interpretation of a previous tracing
- (b) in vivo experiment

**Spotting (as listed):** drug delivery systems, inhalers, insulin syringe, drip chamber, various tablets.

**Viva voce (as listed):**
- Microteaching (teaching exercise)
- Discussion on dissertation
- Principles of general and systemic pharmacology
- Recent advances in pharmacology and drug therapy

Other rules in the same copy: a thesis must be accepted at least six months before the theory and practical exams. A research publication, a national-index paper and two poster presentations are required for eligibility. The "sent up" criteria require 75% in the internal log-book score.

## 4. Subject-specific competencies

**Source: nmch.ac.in copy (2020), section "SUBJECT SPECIFIC COMPETENCIES" and "SUBJECT SPECIFIC LEARNING OBJECTIVES".**

Headings are quoted exactly. Lists are abbreviated.

**A. COGNITIVE DOMAIN** (25 items). Key items:
1. Apply pharmacological principles to drug mechanisms in all body systems.
2. Pharmacodynamics and pharmacokinetics.
3. Drug-drug interactions.
4. Pathophysiology and its modulation by drugs.
5. Pharmacogenetics and pharmacogenomics.
6. Pharmacoeconomics.
7. Pharmacoepidemiology, including drug utilisation studies.
8. GCP and GLP.
9. Essential medicines.
10. Pharmacovigilance.
11. Biostatistics in drug safety and efficacy evaluation (sample size; scales of measurement; central tendency; dispersion; choice of significance tests; correlation and regression; systematic reviews and meta-analysis).
12. Evaluating preclinical and clinical data in drug discovery.
13. Immunology integrated with biochemistry.
14. Research methodology: protocol, data analysis with software, dissemination.
15. Teaching-learning methods: lectures, PBL, case discussions, seminars, journal club.
16. Computer-assisted learning software.
17. Principles of instrumentation.
18. Recent advances in pharmacology and clinical pharmacology.
19. Generic drugs and generic prescribing.
20. Rational drug use and prescription auditing.
21. Antimicrobial stewardship and resistance containment.
22. Animal toxicity studies.
23. Common poisoning.
24. Legal and ethical issues in drug development and research.
25. (Biostatistics items as in 11.)

**B. AFFECTIVE DOMAIN** (6 items): explaining drug effects and adherence to patients; effective communication on rational drug use and adverse-event reporting; respect with peers and health staff; ethical behaviour; creating awareness of generic drugs; self-directed lifelong learning.

**C. PSYCHOMOTOR DOMAIN** (8 items): predicting efficacy and adverse effects with causality assessment; prescription writing; major in vivo and in vitro animal experiments; understanding HPLC; SOPs for clinical trials; determining common poisons in blood; presentation and grant-writing skills; critical appraisal of research papers.

**Practical skills by end of course** (same section, 12 items): in vivo and ex vivo experiments (organ bath, analgesiometer, physiograph, convulsiometer, plethysmograph, learning and memory, affective-disorder models); drug administration by SC, IV, IP routes and oral gavage; dose-response curves on isolated rabbit, rat or guinea-pig intestine and rat uterus; EC50, ED50, pD2 and pA2; mydriatic and miotic effects on rabbit eye; antiepileptic, analgesic, and learning/memory models; toxic drug levels by chemical and biological tests; clinical trial protocol, informed consent and participant information sheet; SAE reporting; evaluating promotional literature; drug information sheets (WHO criteria); bioavailability interpretation; ADR causality and PvPI reporting.
